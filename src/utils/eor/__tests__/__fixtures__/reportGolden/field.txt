Limits of this analysis
- Screening shortlists candidate methods; it does not design a process or predict recovery (Part 2, Summary). A method that qualifies needs
laboratory work, simulation and economics before any decision.
- The criteria come from the oil and reservoir properties of field projects up to 1996 and the displacement mechanisms behind them. Limits are not
sharp in nature: the paper says a value just past a limit does not make a method impossible (Part 1, p. 192). Here a value on a limit passes;
nothing is interpolated between methods.
- Verdicts are pass, marginal, fail or not screened. Marginal means the paper itself softens the limit: a preferred formation (Part 2, Tables 4 and 5),
a carbonate fracture sweep (Part 1, Table 3, note b), or a formation the table does not name for gas injection.
- The CO2 minimum depth by oil gravity is for typical Permian Basin oils; oils of different composition need a measured minimum miscibility
pressure. The CO2 MMP check beside it uses one published correlation (Zhu et al. 2025, from Ordos Basin black oils): it states miscible or
immiscible against the reservoir pressure and changes no Taber verdict.
- Each input is one value for the reservoir. Heterogeneity, layering, dip, vertical permeability and fractures are not screened; the geometry notes of
the gas methods ("thin unless dipping") are printed and not scored.
- Viscosity is the oil viscosity at reservoir conditions. Transmissibility k h / mu (notes c and d) is computed from the permeability, net thickness and
that viscosity.
- Criteria are compared in oilfield units, as published; values typed or shown in SI are converted first.

Flags on the inputs
- CO2 MMP: the reservoir pressure is within the error of the correlation; a measured slim-tube MMP is needed to decide miscibility.
