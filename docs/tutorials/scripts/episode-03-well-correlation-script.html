Before you record

  1. You need at least three wells with logs. Four is better, because the section only starts to read like a section once there is something in the middle. Use the well from Episodes 1 and 2 as one of them so the series stays continuous.
  2. Include one well shared by somebody else. Section 08 shows that renaming and deleting act only on wells you own, and that the status reports the shared ones it left alone. Without a shared well in the section, that beat has nothing to point at. Ask a colleague to share one before the take.
  3. Know your marker before you start. Section 05 picks a top across every well by eye. Choose a marker with clear log character, and find it on each well ahead of the take, so the recording is a demonstration rather than a search.
The argument this episode is making. A top picked here is a registry row, not a drawing on a picture. It is stated in section 01, proved in 05 when a drag writes the depth back, proved again in 08 when a rename crosses every well you own and stops at wells you do not, and paid off in 09 when one click turns the top into a structure map. Protect those four beats above all others.
If you need to reach sixteen minutes. Cut section 07 on zone fills, and cut the propagate beats at the head of section 08. Both are conveniences. Neither carries the argument.

01

Why a section, and why these tops

00:00 · 1:14 · 185 w
Screen
Voice over

Dashboard, Geoscience module. Hold on the Well Correlation tile, then open it.

A single well tells you what is under one point on the map. A cross section tells you what the rocks are doing between those points. Well Correlation is where you build one.

A finished section, four wells, tops correlated and flattened. Hold for four seconds.

It reads the same registry wells as everything else in the Suite, draws them as real log columns side by side, and lets you correlate your tops across them by eye. And here is the part that matters most. The tops you pick here are not drawings on a picture. They are registry rows. Pick one on this screen and it exists immediately in Petrophysics Studio, in Well Data Manager, in Mapping and Surface Studio, and in Seismolord.

Quick cuts, two seconds each: the section path drawing on the map, a top tag being dragged, the section flattening, Mapping and Surface Studio opening on a structure map.

In this video we will build a section from the map, pick and correlate a set of tops, flatten on one of them, fill the zones, and send a top out to be mapped. One correlation, done once, that the whole team then works from.

02

Build the section path

01:14 · 1:50 · 275 w
Screen
Voice over

Open the app on an empty section. Hold on the centre message, then move to the explorer map.

The app opens empty, and it tells you what to do about it. Everything starts on the left, on the map.

Zoom the map panel. Point at a private well then a shared well .

That map shows every well in the registry at its surface location. Amber dots are your private wells. Green dots are wells shared with your organization. You can correlate both.

Click four wells in order. Each turns cyan and numbers itself, and the path line draws between them.

Click wells in the order you want to walk them. Each one you click joins the section, takes a number, and a line draws between them. That line is your section path. Click along a dip direction and you get a dip section. Click along strike and you get a strike section. The geometry is yours to choose, and the map is where you choose it.

The ordered list below the map. Swap two wells with the up and down arrows. Remove one with the cross, then add it back from the Available list.

Underneath, the same wells appear as an ordered list. The arrows move a well along the section. The cross removes it. And every registry well you have not used yet sits in the Available list below, with a plus beside it, in case you would rather add wells from a list than from a map.

Cut to the section drawing in the centre. Then point at the well and top counts on the status bar.

As soon as there are two wells, the section draws. And the status bar keeps a running count of how many wells and how many distinct top names are in play.

03

Reading the section

03:04 · 1:52 · 280 w
Screen
Voice over

Move slowly down one well column: the name band, the track headers with their scales, then the curves.

Each well is a full log column, not a thumbnail. The well name sits in a band at the top. Under it are the track headers with their scales, and then the curves themselves, drawn exactly as Petrophysics Studio would draw them, because both applications share the same painter.

Dock, View, the template selector. Switch to Raw quicklook, then Lithology quicklook, then back to Standard triple combo.

What each column shows comes from a track template, and you choose it once for the whole section, in the dock under View. Standard triple combo gives you gamma ray, resistivity and the density neutron overlay. Raw quicklook draws every mnemonic the well carries. Lithology quicklook adds the gamma ray ramp and its cutoff.

Open the Track layout section at the foot of the dock. Show the editor for three seconds, then collapse it.

And if none of the three is right, the track layout editor is at the bottom of the dock. It is the same editor you used in Petrophysics Studio, so anything you can build there you can build here.

Move the crosshair across the section and hold, so the readouts on every column can be read. Then wheel zoom and drag pan.

Move your cursor and a crosshair runs across every well at once, with each column reporting its own values at that level. The wheel zooms and dragging pans, exactly as in the Studio.

Point at a correlation line joining the same top across two adjacent columns.

And where two wells carry a top with the same name, a line is drawn between them, column centre to column centre. Those lines are your correlation. Everything else in this application exists to help you get them right.

04

Depth reference, unit and spacing

04:56 · 1:40 · 250 w
Screen
Voice over

Dock, View block. Point at the four controls in turn without changing them.

Before you correlate anything, settle three questions in the View block. They change what you are looking at more than anything else here.

Switch unit from m to ft and back. Let the depth axis relabel.

First, the unit. Metres or feet, for display. Storage stays in metres either way.

Switch depth from MD to TVD, then to TVDSS. Hold on each so the columns can be seen shifting.

Second, the depth reference. Measured depth is what the logging tool recorded. True vertical depth removes the deviation. True vertical depth subsea removes the deviation and the kelly bushing, so every well is referenced to the same datum. On a section with deviated wells, or with wells whose rig floors sat at very different elevations, that third option is usually the honest one, because it is the only one where a flat line on the screen means a flat surface in the ground.

Switch spacing from equal to by distance. Hold so the changed gaps between columns are obvious.

Third, spacing. Equal puts the columns at even intervals, which is easy to read. By distance spaces them by their real separation on the map, which is honest about the geometry. Use equal while you are picking, and switch to by distance before you show the picture to anybody.

05

Pick and correlate the tops

06:36 · 2:55 · 430 w
Screen
Voice over

Dock, Tops block, showing the empty state message.

Now the work itself. Open the Tops block in the dock.

Click Pick top. The button changes to Picking… (Esc).

Click Pick top and the cursor is armed. The button tells you it is armed, and it tells you that Escape gets you out again.

Click on the first well column at the marker depth. The naming popover opens. Type the top name and confirm.

Click on a well column at the depth you want, and a popover asks you to name the top. Name it carefully, because that name is the correlation. Every well carrying a top with exactly this name will be joined to this one by a line.

Pause 3 seconds. The top lands and its tag appears on the column.

Pick the same name on the second, third and fourth wells. Let each correlation line draw before moving on.

Now pick that same name on the next well, and the next. Watch the line appear each time. This is the moment your section stops being three logs standing beside each other and becomes a correlation.

Grab a top by its name tag at the right edge of its column and drag it up or down. Release. Hold on the status bar message.

If a pick is not quite right, you do not need to delete it and start again. Grab the top by its name tag at the right edge of its column and drag it. On release the depth updates, and the registry row moves with it. The status bar tells you exactly where it landed.

Dock, Ghost curve. Choose a source well, then a target well. The translucent curve appears over the target column.

There is one more tool here worth knowing, and it is the one experienced correlators reach for first. The ghost curve. Choose a source well and a target well, and the source well's first track is drawn translucent on top of the target's, in the same track slot.

Drag the shift slider slowly until the ghost lines up on a distinctive piece of log character. Hold there.

Then drag the slider to shift it up or down. Now you are not comparing two logs across a gap. You are sliding one log over the other until the character lines up, and reading the offset straight off the slider. It is the fastest honest way to judge whether two wiggles really are the same bed.

Optional, only if a section well can produce it: in TVD or TVDSS, drag a top on a well that reaches the same level twice. Hold on the status notice. Skip if it cannot be reproduced.

One warning you may meet. If you are working in true vertical depth on a well that reaches the same level twice, the app will not guess which one you meant. It says so, and asks you to drag in measured depth instead.

06

Flatten, and stretch

09:31 · 2:48 · 410 w
Screen
Voice over

Dock, Datum block at the top, showing Structural (true depth).

You have a correlation. Now use it. The Datum block at the top of the dock decides how the section hangs.

Wide shot of the structural section. Hold.

Structural is what you have been looking at. Every well at its true depth. This is the picture that tells you about structure. Where the section is high, and where it is low.

Choose Flatten on top and pick the marker. The whole section rearranges so that top becomes one horizontal line.

Choose Flatten on top and pick one of your tops, and every well shifts until that top sits on a single horizontal line.

Pause 4 seconds. Hold on the flattened section. This is the money shot of the episode.

Stay wide. Move the cursor between two wells to compare thicknesses above the datum.

The structure has gone out of the picture, deliberately, and what is left is the stratigraphy. Thickness changes. Character changes. What came in above, and what was already there below. This is the picture that tells you about deposition.

Change the datum depth value in the box beside the top selector. The flat line moves up and down the window.

The depth box beside it simply decides where on the screen that flat line sits.

If a section well lacks the datum top, point at it staying unshifted, and at the status naming it.

A well that does not carry the datum top cannot be flattened on it, so it stays where it is, and the status names it rather than leaving you to notice.

Choose Stretch between two tops. Pick an upper and a lower top. The section rearranges again. Then toggle back to Flatten on top and forward again, to compare.

The third mode goes further. Stretch between two tops hangs each well on two surfaces instead of one. The interval between them is stretched or squeezed until both picks land on common lines, and the rock above and below rides along with it. That is a strong assumption, so know what you are assuming. Flattening on one top says that top was flat when it formed. Stretching between two says the whole interval was laid down over the same span of time everywhere, and only its thickness varied. Where that is a fair thing to say, the stretched section is the clearest picture of facies you will get. Where it is not, it will show you patterns that were never there.

07

Zone fills

12:19 · 1:06 · 165 w
Screen
Voice over

Dock, Zones block. Open the selector so all three options are readable.

A little colour makes a section far easier to read. The Zones block has three settings.

Choose between shown tops and hold as the bands appear. Then choose one pair and select two tops.

No fill leaves you with tops only. Between shown tops puts a band between every consecutive pair of the tops you are showing, coloured by the upper one. One pair fills a single interval that you choose, which is what you want when one reservoir is the subject and everything else is context.

Back to between shown tops. Untick a top in the tops list and hold as the bands either side merge. Tick it again.

And notice that the fills follow the tops you are showing. Untick a top in the list and the bands either side of it merge into one. So those show toggles are not just tidiness. They decide what the coloured picture is saying.

08

Tops across the whole section

13:25 · 2:20 · 350 w
Screen
Voice over

Dock, Propagate top. Type a name and a depth, click Add. Tags appear on every owned well at that same depth.

Picking a top well by well is right when you can see it well by well. Sometimes you would rather start from a first guess. The Propagate block takes a name and a depth, and seeds that top on every well in the section you own, all at that same depth.

Drag each seeded tag in turn to where the marker actually sits. Show the correlation line straightening as you go.

Which is almost certainly wrong on most of them, and that is the point. You now have a tag on every column to drag to where it belongs. It is much faster than picking from nothing, as long as you actually do the dragging.

Tops list. Click the pencil beside a top, type a new name, press Enter. Hold on the status bar count.

In the tops list, the pencil renames a top. It renames it on every well in the section that you own, in one action, and the status tells you how many wells it touched.

Click the bin beside a disposable top. It changes to confirm delete. Wait a beat, then click again.

The bin deletes one, and it asks twice. The first click turns into a confirm, and only the second click does anything at all.

With the shared well in the section, rename a top it also carries. Zoom the status message naming the shared wells left unchanged.

Both of those act only on the wells you own. If your section includes a well somebody shared with you, their copy of that top stays exactly as it is, and the status says how many wells it left alone. You cannot quietly edit someone else's data from here.

Click Reload. Hold on the confirmation in the status bar.

And Reload pulls the tops fresh from the registry. Use it when a colleague has been picking in Petrophysics Studio or Well Data Manager while you have had this section open.

Save, export, and where the tops go

15:45 · 1:55 · 280 w

Voice over

Click Save section on the ribbon. Hold on the status confirmation.

Two buttons on the ribbon finish the job. Save section stores this section as you have it. The wells and their order, the datum you chose, the template, the depth reference, the unit, the spacing, the zone fills, and which tops you are showing. Open it later and you are back exactly where you left off.

Set spacing to by distance, then click PNG. Show the downloaded file, then open it full screen.

PNG downloads the section as an image, ready for a report or a presentation, at the size you have it on screen. Set your spacing to by distance before you press it.

Tops list. Click the small map icon beside the marker.

And then there is the small map icon beside every top in the list.

Pause 3 seconds Mapping and Surface Studio opens on the gridded surface.

Hold on the structure map in Mapping and Surface Studio, then cut back to the section.

Click it, and Mapping and Surface Studio opens with that top gridded as a structure map in true vertical depth subsea, across every well in your section that carries it. Your correlation becomes a surface, in one click, with no export in between.

Cut to Petrophysics Studio on one of the section wells, showing the tops present. Then Well Data Manager, Tops tab, same well.

The same is true everywhere else. Open one of these wells in Petrophysics Studio and your tops are already there, ready to become zone boundaries. Open it in Well Data Manager and they are sitting in its tops tab. You picked them once.
